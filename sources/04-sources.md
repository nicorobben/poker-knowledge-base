## Tier 2 — Books and published-author work

### 1. Tendler, Jared & Carter, Barry — *The Mental Game of Poker: Proven Strategies for Improving Tilt Control, Confidence, Motivation, Coping with Variance, and More* (self-published, 2011)
- **URL (author):** <https://jaredtendler.com/books/the-mental-game-of-poker/>
- **Internet Archive (read in browser):** <https://archive.org/details/mentalgameofpoke0000tend>
- **Tier:** 2
- **Scope:** The canonical modern reference on poker mental game. Introduces the seven tilt profiles (running-bad, injustice, hate-losing, mistake, entitlement, revenge, desperation), the *Adult Learning Model*, and the *Inchworm* A-game / C-game progression model. *Primary source for §2 (Tilt taxonomy), §3 (A-game/C-game), §6 (Downswing playbook), and the anti-pattern table in §14.*
- **How it's used:** All tilt-profile claims and the named concept of "Inchworm" trace back to Tendler. The seven profiles are quoted as his taxonomy, not as folk categorization. Per Standing Rule #8, the book itself is not reproduced — the named profiles and the operational questions are cited, not paraphrased wholesale.

### 2. Tendler, Jared & Carter, Barry — *The Mental Game of Poker 2: Proven Strategies for Improving Poker Skill, Increasing Mental Endurance, and Playing in the Zone Consistently* (self-published, 2013)
- **URL (author):** <https://jaredtendler.com/books/the-mental-game-of-poker-2/>
- **Tier:** 2
- **Scope:** Sequel focused on the learning / flow-state axis rather than the tilt axis. The core operational content is *playing in the Zone*, a structured treatment of flow for poker specifically. Used in §3 (A-game progression), §8 (Session structure), §9 (Pre/post session routines).

### 3. Angelo, Tommy — *Elements of Poker* (self-published, 2007)
- **URL (author):** <https://www.tommyangelo.com/elements-of-poker/>
- **Publisher listing:** <https://www.amazon.com/Elements-Poker-Tommy-Angelo/dp/1419680897>
- **Tier:** 2
- **Scope:** 144-lesson culmination of Angelo's coaching work on the non-technical side of poker. Introduces the concepts of *reciprocality* (profit comes from doing things differently from opponents — in all aspects of play, not just strategy), *tilt reciprocality* (you don't need to be tiltless, you just need to tilt less than the field), and structured treatments of session management and self-observation. *Primary source for §1 (Why mental game is the biggest leak), §5 (Variance acceptance), and §8–9 (Session structure, routines).*

### 4. Miller, Ed — *Poker's 1%: The One Big Secret That Keeps Elite Players On Top* (Notzark Publishing, 2014)
- **URL:** <https://www.amazon.com/Pokers-1-Secret-Keeps-Players/dp/1496159187>
- **Internet Archive (read in browser):** <https://archive.org/details/pokers1onebigsec0000edmi>
- **Tier:** 2
- **Scope:** Miller's argument that the top 1% of poker players separate themselves through *study habits and frequency-based thinking*, not talent. The motivational-and-study-habits framing underpins §10 (Study habits) and §11 (Ego and confidence calibration — Miller's "insatiable curiosity" framing). Note that Doc 1 already references Miller's broader body of work (*Small Stakes Hold'em*) at L7; this doc picks up the mental/motivational thread specifically.
- **Caveat:** Published pre-solver era (2014). The concrete "frequency work" examples are pre-GTO in vocabulary but the study-methodology framework survives the transition.

### 5. Ericsson, K. Anders & Pool, Robert — *Peak: Secrets from the New Science of Expertise* (Eamon Dolan / Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2016)
- **URL (Wikipedia overview):** <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak:_Secrets_from_the_New_Science_of_Expertise>
- **Publisher listing:** <https://www.amazon.com/Peak-Secrets-New-Science-Expertise/dp/0544456238>
- **Tier:** 2
- **Scope:** The primary scientific treatment of *deliberate practice* — the concept Tendler's Inchworm operationalizes for poker specifically. Used in §10 (Study habits) to source the claim that "structured, feedback-rich, uncomfortable practice" is qualitatively different from hours-played. Ericsson explicitly corrected the "10,000-hour rule" misreading of his work in this book — relevant to the honest framing of how long real improvement takes.

### 6. Chen, Bill & Ankenman, Jerrod — *The Mathematics of Poker* (Conjelco, 2006)
- **URL (publisher):** <https://www.amazon.com/Mathematics-Poker-Bill-Chen/dp/1886070253>
- **Tier:** 2
- **Scope:** Used as an adjunct source for §5 (Variance — emotional acceptance). Chen & Ankenman's variance math (Doc 1 cites them at L7 and L8 as the guest lecturer) grounds the "intellectual acceptance" half of variance; the *emotional* acceptance is downstream of the math being real, not optional. Minor source — only for the mathematical foundation of risk-of-ruin arithmetic behind bankroll guidelines.

## Tier 3 — Training-site and coach-published content

### 7. Roe, Elliot — *elliotroe.com* (personal site, mindset coaching for high performers)
- **URL:** <https://elliotroe.com/>
- **Tier:** 3
- **Scope:** Roe is a mindset coach / clinical hypnotherapist who has worked specifically with high-stakes poker players since ~2013. His public content (podcast appearances, interviews, free Primed Mind audios) is the second-most-cited contemporary mental-game voice in poker after Tendler. Used in §9 (Pre/post session routines) and §11 (Confidence calibration).

### 8. Roe, Elliot — *Primed Mind* (iOS / Android app, hypnotherapy-based mindset audios)
- **URL:** <https://primedmind.com/>
- **Tier:** 3
- **Scope:** Roe's app offering pre-session primers, deep-focus and decompression audios. Referenced in §9 and Doc 08's resources roster (forward cross-ref). Per Standing Rule #7, the app's paid tiers and pricing may drift — framed in this doc as "Roe's methodology; check the app for the current offering (as of April 2026)."
- **Caveat on dual-use:** Primed Mind is a *tool* recommendation. This doc does not make it a requirement — Tendler's book-level material and Angelo's session practices cover the same ground with different methods.

### 9. Brokos, Andrew & (formerly) Meyvis, Nate — *Thinking Poker* podcast
- **URL:** <https://www.thinkingpoker.net/>
- **Apple Podcasts:** <https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/thinking-poker/id564288259>
- **Tier:** 3

Cited as a background source for §1 (culture of the game), §7 (Upswing playbook — several episodes treat overconfidence creep directly), and §13 (Diagnostic: technical-vs-mental). ### 10. PokerStars Learn — *Seven Types of Tilt* (vendor-published summary of Tendler)

## Tier 4 — Author blogs and practitioner essays

### 11. Tendler, Jared — *The Inchworm Concept* (jaredtendler.com)
- **URL:** <https://jaredtendler.com/the-inchworm-concept/>
- **Tier:** 4 (author-primary, publicly accessible)
- **Scope:** Tendler's own public write-up of the Inchworm concept — *improvement happens from both ends of the bell curve simultaneously (A-game moves forward, C-game moves forward)*. This free essay is the best open-web source for the concept when citing it to a student who doesn't own the book.

### 12. Tendler, Jared — *Learning More Efficiently* (jaredtendler.com)
- **URL:** <https://jaredtendler.com/learning-more-efficiently/>
- **Tier:** 4
- **Scope:** Tendler's public essay on study structure and the *Adult Learning Model* (ALM) — the idea that mental-game improvement needs direct, honest confrontation with the problem, not avoidance. Used in §10 (Study habits).

### 13. Tendler, Jared — *Mental Game of Poker Excerpt 4 — Tilt Profile* (Hendon Mob publication of book excerpt)
- **URL:** <https://www.thehendonmob.com/jared_tendler/mental_game_of_poker_excerpt_4_tilt_profile>
- **Tier:** 4 (publisher-republished book excerpt)
- **Scope:** A publicly available excerpt of the tilt-profile chapter from Tendler 2011. Useful when linking a student to primary material without requiring them to buy the book.

### 14. Angelo, Tommy — *Reciprocality: The Cause of Profit at Poker* (tommyangelo.com)
- **URL:** <https://www.tommyangelo.com/reciprocality/>

## Notes on sourcing gaps and honest disclosure

Per the house-style rule (BUILD_PLAN §2.3, Standing Rule #3) and the user's Phase 1 instruction to *"name the gap explicitly rather than reconstructing from adjacent data"*, three things need to be flagged up front before Phase 3 drafting begins:

**(a) The Shahade "Goldilocks" reference.** Doc 1's L7 notes credit Jennifer Shahade's L5 guest lecture (unrecorded by OCW) for the "life as one long session" framing, and specifically mentions her *Goldilocks* video. Web search confirms the *Goldilocks* project exists as a film by Shahade and Daniel Meirom exploring gender dynamics in chess and poker (<https://jennifershahade.com/poker/>), but does **not** surface a publicly linkable clip of the specific "life as one long session" argument, and Doc 1's citation is itself a second-hand reference to an unrecorded lecture. This doc will use the framing, credit it to Shahade via Will Ma's L7 attribution, and not invent a specific public clip to cite.

**(b) Specific bankroll number guidelines.** Community sources (BlackRain79, PokerStrategy, Tournament Poker Edge, etc.) give broadly consistent ranges — cash games 20–50 buy-ins, MTTs 100–300 buy-ins, SNGs 30–100 — but there is no single authoritative *primary* source. Tendler, Angelo, and Miller all treat bankroll management but in narrative terms, not with a single canonical number. Doc 04 will present the ranges *as community consensus* with the caveat that the "right" number is a risk-tolerance question, not a solved one. No derivative-extrapolation from Will Ma's 2016 framing is attempted (his L7 closing does not provide bankroll numbers). This is the same pattern as Doc 02's handling of opening-range gaps.

**(c) The David Einhorn $1M tournament story.** Will Ma narrates this in L7 as a calibration example. Einhorn's actual 3rd-place finish in the 2012 *WSOP One Drop* ($18M prize pool) is well-documented in contemporary coverage; Doc 04 will reproduce the anecdote as Will Ma told it (with attribution to L7) rather than re-researching event details, because the point of the anecdote is the *calibration story*, not the tournament facts.
